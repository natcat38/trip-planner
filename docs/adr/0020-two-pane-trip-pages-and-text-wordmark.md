# ADR-0020: Two-pane trip pages and a text-wordmark identity

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-23)

Owner decisions from the 2026-10 UX polish pass (portfolio-wide calls G2 and G3, trip calls D1,
D2 and D3). Shipped in #63, #64, #65 and #66.

## Context
A UX audit on 2026-10-23 measured the two pages a visitor actually reads:

- The **shared view** (`/shared/[token]`, the page a recruiter opens) was one 768px column on a
  1440px screen. The budget card and a 576px map came first, so the first day sat 1.4 screens
  down on desktop. The page was 5.2 screens long on desktop and 6.1 on a phone, the map scrolled
  away while reading, and there was no header or product name.
- The **signed-in trip page** put the budget form and the sharing panel before the itinerary.
  The first day sat 1.2 screens down on desktop and 2 on a phone; the page was 9.7 / 13.5
  screens with 272 Tab stops.
- No page carried a logo or an Open Graph tag, so a pasted link showed a bare URL.

ADR-0019 set the palette, type and "departure board" treatment. It did not decide page structure
or identity; this ADR does.

## Decision

### 1. Two panes from `lg` on both trip pages (G2 A)
- **Shared view (D1 A):** itinerary on the left, the map sticky on the right; the budget is one
  slim summary bar with the breakdown collapsed; a sticky day-chip strip. On a phone: the budget
  bar, then the itinerary, with the map behind a "Map" toggle.
- **Signed-in trip page (D2 C on desktop, D2 B on a phone):** the itinerary on the left; the
  budget summary, a one-line sharing summary and the sticky map in a 26rem right column
  (`TripLayout`). Checklist and Attachments sit under the itinerary. On a phone it is one column:
  budget bar, map, itinerary, then Sharing, Checklist and Attachments as collapsed sections.
- Both pages share one container module (`src/components/pageShell.ts`) with their loading
  skeletons, and the signed-in header row follows the page width.

### 2. Identity is a text wordmark plus a generated share card (G3 A)
- "Trip Planner" in Geist and the existing `--accent` (`Wordmark` component) in the shared
  header and the signed-in header. No logo mark.
- A 1200x630 share card generated by `next/og` (`src/lib/ogCard.tsx`) from the wordmark and the
  site's existing one-line description, served at `/opengraph-image` and `/twitter-image`.
  Titles follow `%s · Trip Planner`.

### 3. Left open by the owner
- The call-to-action copy ("View a sample trip" / "Plan your own trip") for the landing page and
  the shared header. A `TODO(Natalie)` marks the spot in `SharedTripView.tsx`.
- A branded sign-in page (the stock Auth.js page stays until the copy above is decided).

## Alternatives considered
- **D1 B, one wider column with a sticky map band:** closest to the old feel, but still 4-5
  screens and the map takes vertical space.
- **D1 C / D2 A, tabs (Itinerary | Map | Budget):** the shortest pages, but hides the budget,
  which is a selling point, behind a tap.
- **D2 B on desktop too (reorder, collapse):** smallest change, but still about 8 screens.
- **G3 B, a logo mark:** better recognition, but needs a mark the owner supplies; an agent must
  not invent one.
- **G3 C, per-trip share images with a map thumbnail:** the best link preview, but the most work
  and needs a Mapbox static-image source.

## Consequences
- Shared view: 5.2 -> 4.1 screens on desktop, 6.1 -> 5.4 on a phone; first day heading y=1284 ->
  318 on desktop.
- Signed-in trip page: 10.3 -> 6.4 screens on desktop, 15.2 -> 8.2 on a phone; Tab stops
  671 -> 254.
- Every page has `og:` and `twitter:` tags (0 -> 9 `og:` tags) and a share card.
- Harder: the map competes with the itinerary for width at `lg`, and the selection state now
  lives in a client `TripLayout` around server components. The intermittent server-action
  stall tracked in #56 shows up slightly more often on this structure.
- Changing the description or the accent means updating `siteMeta.ts` and the colour literals in
  `ogCard.tsx` (an image cannot read CSS variables).
